Travel search widget redesign

Modernizing the booking entry point across desktop and mobile.

User experience Design system Visual design

Key results

37% increase in clicks

17.77% increase in bookings

The challenge

The control search widget was the entry point to every travel booking, but its design worked against the task. Six product tabs rendered as heavy bordered cards with stacked icons and labels, consuming a full band of vertical space before a member could reach a single input field. On mobile the tab row overflowed horizontally, cutting off Activities entirely and leaving Cruises partially hidden — two products effectively invisible to anyone who didn't think to scroll sideways.

A promotional headline sat between the tabs and the form, pushing the fields further down. Below it, an outlined field treatment gave every input the same visual weight, so nothing signaled where to start.

The solution

Rebuilt the widget around speed to first input.

Product tabs were flattened from bordered cards into inline text-and-icon links, with the active state carried by a single filled pill. This cut the tab band's height substantially and let all six products fit on one line on desktop. On mobile, the overflow was resolved with a visible forward affordance so members could tell more products existed rather than discovering them by accident.

The promotional headline was removed. It described a benefit members had already accepted by arriving at the widget, and it delayed the task.

Fields moved from outlined to filled treatment, creating a lighter, more scannable form where the input areas read as a connected group. On mobile, the search button was promoted directly beneath the fields, with Advanced options and My reservations demoted below it.

Conclusion

Clicks rose 37% and bookings 17.77% with no change to inventory, pricing, or the underlying booking flow. The lift came from removing what sat between the member and the first field: a heavy tab band, a redundant headline, and a mobile layout that hid two of six products.

The gap between the two numbers is itself informative. Clicks grew faster than bookings, which is what you'd expect when a redesign primarily improves discoverability: more members engaged with more products, including ones they previously couldn't see.

Credit card dual page experience redesign

Driving higher application and account conversion through personalized credit card experiences

Visual design Personalization A/B testing Information architecture Conversion optimization

Key results

Phase 1: Targeted audiences: 26% and 11% application start rates; 2.49% and 3.40% new account conversion.

Phase 2: General traffic: +10.2% application starts, +9.6% completions, +11.3% net new accounts.

The challenge

The organization offered two different credit card products, but the existing credit card experience was not optimized for different member needs and intent. A single, undifferentiated page asked every member — whether travel-motivated, everyday-rewards-motivated, or still comparing — to self-select their way to the right product.

The goal was to create more relevant and engaging experiences that would increase credit card awareness, application starts, and new account acquisition.

The solution

Three intent-based experiences: Dual Card for members evaluating both card options, Travel Card for travel-focused members, and Daily Card for everyday rewards. Each with clearer value props, stronger content hierarchy, and CTAs matched to member intent.

Phase 1: Targeted audience validation

Tested with two high-intent segments.

Members with prior card, travel, or rewards engagement started applications at 26% and converted to new accounts at 2.49%. Members pre-screened for eligibility started at 11% but converted at 3.40% — the highest account rate in the program.

Takeaway: Interest drives starts. Eligibility drives completions. Both confirmed that matching design to intent works, but not whether the design alone deserved credit.

Phase 2: Head-to-head vs. original experience

Same design, general traffic, direct A/B test against the existing page.

Application start rate rose from 4.48% to 4.94%, a lift of 10.2%. Application completion rate rose from 3.22% to 3.53%, up 9.6%. Net account rate rose from 2.0% to 2.2%, up 11.3%.

Takeaway: The lift held down the entire funnel, and the biggest gain landed on new accounts, which is the metric closest to revenue. No offer or product changed. Only the experience did.

Conclusion

Phase 1 proved relevance drives engagement. Phase 2 proved the design carried the lift on its own, without targeting.

Targeting is capped by audience size, but experience improvements scale across all traffic. The pattern is validated and ready to extend to the Travel and Daily experiences.

Account recovery experience

Simplifying a high-friction account recovery journey

UX design Content design Interaction design User flows Analytics

Key results

Username recovery: up 54%. More members successfully recovered their username.

Membership-number path: up 154%. Members who didn't know their username now had a way through, and used it.

The challenge

Recovering an account can be a frustrating experience, especially when members don't remember the username associated with their account.

The existing experience needed a clearer path for members to verify their identity and recover access, while also supporting those who didn't know their username.

The goal was to simplify the journey, reduce friction, and give members a clear way forward at every step.

The approach

I designed an account recovery experience with two entry points for identity verification.

Username path: Members who remember their username can enter it to begin the recovery process. After verifying their identity with a one-time PIN, they can create a new password and regain access to their account.

Membership number path: Members who don't remember their username can use their membership number and ZIP code instead. After verification, they receive their username and can continue through the password reset process.

Both paths ultimately lead members through the same streamlined password recovery experience.

The solution

Key improvements included: providing an alternative to username-based recovery, breaking the recovery process into simple, manageable steps, clearly communicating what information members need at each stage, using a one-time PIN to verify member identity, providing clear feedback when a username is recovered or a password is successfully changed, and creating a consistent experience across both recovery paths.

The final experience

The resulting flow guides members through: Choose recovery method → Verify identity → Recover username if needed → Create new password → Confirmation.

This approach ensures that forgetting a username doesn't become a dead end and gives members a clear path back into their account.

My account login experience redesign

Reducing login friction and improving account access conversion.

UX design Conversion optimization Form design Competitive analysis Information architecture

Key results

Forgot username: 60% drop in credential-recovery clicks

Get proof of insurance: click-through nearly doubled, 0.9% to 1.7%

Login conversion: 52%, a 3% relative lift

My Account visits: up 5%

The challenge

The login page was a plain form beside an undifferentiated list of "skip login" links. Members who couldn't remember their credentials had one visible escape hatch, and self-service tasks were buried in a flat link list with no grouping or hierarchy. The page also sat outside the main site navigation, dead-ending anyone who arrived by mistake.

The solution

Rebuilt the page around two clear paths: log in, or complete a task without logging in.

Login moved into a contained card with clear focus. Skip-login tasks were grouped under Membership and Insurance headers and converted from text links into scannable buttons. Primary navigation was added so members could reach other parts of the site. Join AAA was folded into the login card as a tertiary path rather than a competing column.

Results

Measured across matched 135-day pre and post periods.

Login conversion rose from 50% to 52%, a 3% relative lift. Login page visits grew 2% and My Account visits grew 5%.

Forgot username click-through dropped from 11.6% to 4.6%, a 60% decline. This is the headline result, since the recovery link is a failure signal.

Get proof of insurance click-through rose from 0.9% to 1.7%, nearly doubling. This is evidence that grouping tasks by category improved discoverability for high-intent needs.

Conclusion

The redesign moved members through the front door rather than around it. Login conversion improved while credential-recovery clicks fell by more than half.

The one skip-login task that grew, proof of insurance, points to where categorization pays off: grouped and labeled buttons outperform an undifferentiated link list.

Car buying service redesign

Rebuilding the Locate a Dealer experience around member pricing

Competitive analysis Information architecture

Key results

After implementation, the page saw a 66.43% increase in referrals over the previous 3-month average.

The challenge

The dealer referral form was structured around the system's needs, not the member's. Three numbered steps front-loaded vehicle selection before explaining what the member would get. Membership validation sat in the middle of the flow as a hard gate. Dense terms and conditions ran below the form with no answers to the questions members actually had.

The solution

Rebuilt the page around the member's decision, not the form's sequence.

The headline was rewritten to lead with the benefit: member pricing at a recommended dealership. "How it works" moved up beside a supporting image and expanded into a plain-language five-step summary.

The rigid Step 1/2/3 structure was replaced with two clearly labeled sections: vehicle details and contact information. Membership verification became a persistent inline banner instead of a blocking wall mid-form.

Below the form, new content did work the original page left undone: a benefits section with tabs for new and used car purchases, and an FAQ addressing the six questions members most often asked before submitting.

Conclusion

Referrals rose 66.43% without any change to the underlying offer or dealer network. The gain came from restructuring the page around member confidence: explaining the value before asking for information, removing the mid-form gate, and answering objections on the page.

ZIP code modal redesign

Turning a trust barrier into a welcome.

Illustration Brand design AI-assisted design UX writing Motion design

Outcome

Spam complaints stopped. The modal went from a trust barrier to a branded moment, and the artwork became a recurring element across the site.

The challenge

The ZIP code modal was the first thing many members encountered, and users said it felt like spam.

The reason was structural. The screen had every marker of a phishing interstitial: it appeared before any content, asked for personal information with no explanation of why, and gave no visual signal that it belonged to a real, familiar brand.

The modal existed for a legitimate reason: AAA is a federation of independent clubs, and the ZIP determines which club's site a member sees. But the design gave members no reason to trust it.

The solution

Rebuilt the modal to signal warmth and legitimacy before asking for anything.

Custom illustration. Original artwork beneath the input, turning a bare form into a branded moment, the one assurance a phishing page can't fake.

Seasonal variation. A set tied to major holidays, so the screen changes through the year and reads as actively maintained.

Scaling with AI. Directed AI tooling to extend the illustration set in my established style.

Beyond the modal. Animated the artwork in Adobe Animate as HTML5 for the homepage, beside the AAA logo.

Conclusion

The complaints stopped. Nothing changed about what the modal asked for or why it existed; what changed was whether members believed it was legitimate.

Trust on a first-touch screen isn't earned through explanation alone; members decide in under a second, before reading.
